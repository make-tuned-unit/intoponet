See the news
where it happens.

intoponet starts from Earth, not a blank tab. Local news from 450 outlets, what Wikipedia readers are opening, and earthquakes and storms show up where they happen, through the day.

  • Story mountains New

    Every story rises as terrain: the more outlets carry it, the higher it stands. Hover a summit for strands to every country that covered it, or press H for the highest.

  • Stories the world is covering

    World ranks the stories outlets in many countries carry at once. Open one and its sources line up as cards, translated on your Mac.

  • Fly anywhere by name

    Type a city, region or country, even misspelled, and the globe turns there and marks the spot. Every country is named and clickable.

  • A country’s bigger picture

    Open a country for its key facts, its recent history and its local headlines.

  • Rising and fading

    See which stories are gaining coverage, which hold attention and which are going quiet.

  • The page behind every signal

    Hover a ripple to preview it, then click to open the article itself in a tab.

Understand the story
behind the headline.

How a story got here, how two outlets tell it, and what a page says in your language.

  • The Olds

    Walks back through how a story got here: dated events from Wikipedia, each linked to its source. Read it as a story, a timeline or a list.

  • Both sides of a debate

    Where Wikipedia attributes competing views, The Olds shows them side by side, with who holds each.

  • An outlet’s own past New

    See what an outlet wrote about a story before, month by month, from its topic pages and the Wayback Machine.

  • Compare two outlets New

    Put two outlets’ articles on the same story side by side, each labelled with its country and translated on its own.

  • Free coverage of paywalled stories

    Behind a paywall? intoponet offers free coverage of the same story from other outlets. It never gets around a paywall.

  • Pages in your language

    Pages and headlines translate on your Mac, automatically or when you ask. Languages without a model translate roughly, and say so.

Organize your tabs
into workspaces.

Keep work, a trip and a story you follow apart. The sidebar holds pinned tabs, folders and open tabs, and it tidies itself.

  • Workspaces New

    Each has its own name, icon, colour, pinned tabs, folders and open tabs. Switch with ⌃1–9.

  • Separate sign-ins New

    A workspace can keep its own cookies, passwords and permissions, so you can be signed in to two accounts on one site.

  • Workspaces anchored to a place New

    Switching to it flies the globe there, and its sidebar offers that place’s news. A place you choose, never your location.

  • Vertical tabs and folders New

    Tabs down the side in every view, in folders that nest and fold away. Pinned tabs remember their home.

  • Tabs that sleep and tidy away New

    Background tabs sleep to save memory and wake where you left them. Tabs you haven’t looked at for a while move to Archived tabs.

  • Group by story and Tidy New

    Articles on the same world story gather into one folder. Tidy suggests folders for the rest, worked out on your Mac.

Browse faster
without losing your place.

Two pages at once, a quick look at a link, a video that follows you, and every command a few keys away.

  • Pages side by side New

    Drag a tab to the edge of the page for two pages in columns. Each side navigates, searches and zooms on its own.

  • Link previews New

    ⇧-click a link to open it in a card over the page. Open it as a tab if you want to stay.

  • Links from other apps New

    Links from Mail or Slack can open in a small quick window, and rules can send a site’s links straight to a workspace.

  • Video follows you New

    Switch away from a playing video and it floats in picture-in-picture. Come back and it docks again.

  • Make a site yours New

    Hide anything on a site by pointing at it, and choose that site’s reading width, font and colours.

  • Commands from the keyboard New

    Press ⌘K to find any command, setting, tab, bookmark or place. ⌃Tab switches tabs in the order you used them.

Keep what you find
on your Mac.

Everything you save remembers where it came from and where it is on Earth. Your library is encrypted on this Mac and never leaves it.

  • Bookmarks with a place

    Save pages into groups with tags and private notes, search all of them, and see them on your own map.

  • Clean PDFs in one click

    Save any page as a tidy, searchable PDF without ads or pop-ups, with its source on page one and links that still work.

  • Screenshots and recordings

    Drag a frame over part of a page to capture a picture or a short video, filed into folders you can open in Finder.

  • Absorb and Laya

    Keep a page, or a passage, in your own knowledge. Ask in your own words and get the passage back, cited. It runs on your Mac.

  • My map

    Pins turn pages into places: a restaurant, a field report, a trail. My map is yours alone.

  • Beacons Private beta

    Share a pin publicly as a Beacon, after seeing exactly what will be public. Nothing is shared unless you choose to.

Switch in a few minutes.
Bring everything.

intoponet imports from Chrome, Arc, Brave, Edge and Vivaldi on this Mac. Nothing is uploaded, and importing twice never adds duplicates.

  1. Open Settings › Import

    intoponet finds the browsers on this Mac and shows what each one can bring.

  2. Tick what you want

    Bookmarks and folders, pinned and open tabs, and if you like your history and passwords. Your Mac asks before passwords are read.

  3. Pick up where you left off

    Arc’s spaces come in as workspaces, with their pinned tabs, nested folders and favourites. Extensions don’t come over: intoponet doesn’t run them.

Privacy

Your curiosity
belongs to you.
Your discoveries
can belong to all of us.

What you read, save and type stays on your Mac.

  • Ads and trackers are blocked on every page, without the blank boxes they leave behind.
  • Translation, Laya and Tidy run on this Mac, so the text they work on never leaves it.
  • Your library, history and saved passwords are encrypted on this Mac.
  • Private browsing leaves no history, and forgets site sign-ins when it ends.
  • A workspace can keep its own sign-ins, apart from everything else.
  • Sites ask before they use your camera, microphone or location, and DNS lookups are encrypted where possible.

Questions

Something missing? Share an idea or ask us.

Does intoponet cost anything?

No. intoponet is free to download and use, with no account needed.

What does “public preview” mean?

Everything on this page works today, and every release passes 63 automated checks that drive the real app before it ships. It is still early, so expect rough edges and frequent updates, and tell us what you find.

Which Macs does intoponet run on?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 13 Ventura or later. Intel Macs aren’t supported. The download is a 120 MB disk image, signed by the developer and notarized by Apple.

Will my websites work?

Yes. intoponet shows pages with Chromium, the engine behind Chrome, Edge and Arc, and sites see it as Chrome.

Can I bring my bookmarks, tabs and passwords?

Yes. Settings imports bookmarks, open and pinned tabs, history and passwords from Chrome, Arc, Brave, Edge or Vivaldi on the same Mac. Arc’s spaces come in as workspaces. You see a preview before anything is imported.

Does intoponet see what I browse?

No. Your history, library, saved passwords and knowledge are kept on your Mac, encrypted, and translation and Laya run on it too. The globe’s news comes from public sources. Looking up a headline in The Olds asks Wikipedia and Wikidata about that headline, and publishing a Beacon shares only what you saw in its preview.

Is Laya a cloud AI?

No. Laya is optional and runs on your Mac. It searches the pages you have absorbed and suggests folders for your tabs, without sending anything anywhere.

Does it support Chrome extensions?

Not at the moment. Ad and tracker protection, translation, PDFs, screenshots and a password manager are built in.

How do updates work?

intoponet checks for a new version once a day (you can turn that off in Settings) and installs it when you restart. Every release is listed under What’s new.

Is there a version for Windows, iPhone or iPad?

Not at the moment. intoponet is a Mac app. On a phone, use “Send this page to your Mac” at the top of this page.

Who makes intoponet?

intoponet is built by Atlas Atlantic. Ideas, questions and bug reports go through Share an idea.
