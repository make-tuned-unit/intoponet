## Bounded improvement

Historical expansion already recognizes earlier editions when a linked background article names them. It did not inspect the current event article itself for those same explicit edition links. That left a systematic gap for dated event-series pages that link to past editions directly but have no separate background article pointer. A second gap appeared when the current page linked only its recent previous edition: that edition could be read, but never as displayed history, to discover exact older links.

The Olds expansion now scans the root article's wikitext for exact same-series earlier-edition titles before following any background-article links. If the current page has fewer than three eligible older editions and explicitly links a more recent prior edition, Olds may read that one page as an index for exact older links. Recent prose is never included as historical context. When an even number of older editions is available, the sample now uses the nearest middle edition rather than biasing to the earlier quarter point. The year-prefixed series name must match exactly, edition-page lookups share a three-page cap (including a failed bridge request), and the normal six-entry historical-context cap and source attribution still apply. Regression checks cover unrelated series links, root-only links, the recent bridge path, balanced sampling, the page budget and attribution.

This uses dated passages from the linked Wikipedia edition pages and labels each with its source title. It does not infer relationships or add general country history. Cache version v18 makes saved threads recompute under the new lookups. In sequential live audits of the same saved threads, v15 had 10 rows with added context and v18 had 13; the 47/80 limited count did not change. Wikipedia is live, so the expansion-count comparison is not a controlled causal result. The bridge path added no audit rows in this 80-thread set, although its service-level mock verifies the bounded path. Of the remaining 47 flags, 18 have broad place roots, 33 have no older dated years under the screen, 11 have one and three have two. The flag counts dates and does not judge the relevance of every pre-existing chronology passage. No generic country or city history is used to pad the remaining gaps.

## Headline-to-history relevance follow-up — 29 October 2026

I spot-checked two place-root examples against the live story sources. The Uniqlo report identifies the subject as Prime Minister Tarique Rahman's request to Uniqlo/Fast Retailing to invest in Bangladesh's ready-made garment sector, with man-made fibre, technical textiles, and sustainable manufacturing named as focus areas ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/pm-urges-japans-uniqlo-expand-investment-bangladesh-4309516), [BSS](https://www.bssnews.net/news-flash/439254)). The saved Olds thread was rooted at Bangladesh, and its six recent entries did not add older sourced context. These articles establish the current story subject but do not themselves justify adding unrelated country history.

The housing headline is more revealing: the live report says the housing proposals are on Alderney, at the former Old Connaught site and Le Val, although the saved thread selected Bailiwick of Guernsey ([AOL / Channel Islands reporting](https://www.aol.com/articles/states-seeks-interest-possible-housing-133410000.html)). Wikidata describes Bailiwick of Guernsey as a British Crown Dependency in the Channel Islands. That is geographic context, but it does not establish that Guernsey's general history is the subject of the headline.

The headline selection path now classifies Crown Dependencies as broad places, gives a specific related subject a chance to replace a broad place match, and labels any remaining broad-place result as a **geographic fallback** in `context.matchedBy`. The country fallback uses the same explicit wording. These messages say that no specific subject or event was verified and that the shown history is place-level. Mocked service regressions cover a Crown Dependency whose Wikidata class is otherwise unknown and a specific event whose description mentions a city and an island. The Olds service tests passed (17 tests) with `npm run test --workspace @intoponet/desktop -- src/main/olds/headlineThread.test.ts src/main/olds/olds.test.ts`; the existing Olds gold selection checks also passed (7 tests) with `node --test experiments/e2e/olds-gold.test.mjs`. Cache version v19 ensures saved headline results recompute this matching explanation.

This is a transparency and broad-place classification fix, not a claim that every headline now resolves to its precise local subject. In the Alderney example, the headline itself does not name Alderney; a source article body or an editor's current-events filing is needed to identify that narrower subject. For the Uniqlo story, specific textile-sector pages may be relevant, but historical relevance must be established from those sources before replacing the country fallback. The last recorded 80-thread depth-screening count remains 47 limited; headline matching was not rerun across the full set after this clarification.
